## The problem you lived

Petteri and Nikita built Inbind out of their own experience: a developer and a product manager who kept hitting the wall when it came to marketing their own work. They had past startup attempts and experienced first-hand the friction of needing content to grow but lacking the right tooling for it. "How our professional careers as a developer and product manager intersect with the marketing needs" is a core communication theme.

## The turning point

## What you tried first

The Google Sheet. Petteri was already managing his Webflow collection content in a spreadsheet — a table of every item with all fields visible — because Webflow's own interface made it too hard to see everything at once. That workaround became the first feature of Inbind.

## What makes this different

Inbind sits on top of the platforms content teams already use — it doesn't replace Webflow or any other frontend. Instead of migrating to a new CMS, teams connect Inbind to their existing setup in minutes. Where other CMS tools are built for developers, Inbind is built specifically for content marketers: SEO fields surface in the editor by default, publishing doesn't require Designer access or a developer, and generated fields handle repetitive metadata automatically.

## Who it's built for

Content marketers and content teams who manage website content on Webflow (and other frontend frameworks) — people who are confident in their content skills but frustrated by tools that weren't built with them in mind. Also agencies who need to hand off safe content editing to clients without risking layout damage.

## Where we're going

A full content operations platform — from brief to published piece, across any format and any channel. Inbind handles the "How" so content teams can own the "What".

## Quick version

Petteri was managing a Webflow site and hit a wall with programmatic SEO: HTML tags in Designer, a Google Sheet for structure, and a manual export/import every time something changed. He turned that spreadsheet into Inbind's first feature. Now it's a content operations platform that handles the publishing workflow end-to-end — so content teams can stop fighting their tools and focus on the work.

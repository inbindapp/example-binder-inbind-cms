# Voice & Tone

> ⚠️ This page was generated from incomplete source material. Review and expand the sections that are missing or thin.

## Brand archetype

The Everyman. From the brand book: "A relatable, down-to-earth, and authentic presence that positions us as a trusted peer to our users. We are the helpful friend who understands the challenges of modern digital work, not the distant corporate entity or the know-it-all expert."

Five personality traits: **Relatable, Sunny, Authentic, Pragmatic, Chill.**

Core brand values: Relatable, Authentic, Helpful, Pragmatic, Humble.

## Voice in three words

Direct. Practical. Empathetic.

## Words and phrases to use

Patterns from the website and brand book:

- "content marketers" — the audience is always named, never just "users"
- "one workspace" / "one place" — emphasis on consolidation
- "without a developer" / "no Developer access needed" — freedom from technical dependency
- "SEO focused" — consistently hyphenated
- "clean editor" / "focused workspace"
- "publish when you're ready" — control in the user's hands
- Action-first CTAs: "Get Started", "Start for Free"
- "Real", "actually", "honestly" — authenticity markers
- "We learned", "we found", "we discovered" — transparency
- "You might", "you could", "you can" — empowering
- "We don't", "we haven't", "we're still working on" — honesty
- Contractions naturally ("we're", "don't", "it's")

## Words and phrases to avoid

From the brand book and inferred from tone:

- "Cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changing" — overused, undermines authenticity
- "Seamless", "effortless", "magical" — overpromises
- "Leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem" — corporate jargon
- Jargon-heavy developer speak ("API endpoints", "webhooks") in marketing copy
- Passive voice where action is the point
- Excessive exclamation marks (one per paragraph maximum)
- Em-dashes — use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead

## Tone across contexts

### Marketing and social media

Conversational and empathetic. Leads with the pain before the solution. Uses short, punchy sentences. Calls out specific frustrations ("You know the drill. Check one tool for drafts, another for metadata..."). Rhetorical observations ("You're a content marketer, not a project manager for your own publishing process.").

### Formal communications and email

"Warm and personal, with genuine care." Address people by name, be concise but not cold. Acknowledge their specific situation. Drop the jargon, keep the warmth.

### Customer support

Empathetic and solution-focused. Acknowledge the frustration before jumping to solutions. Use "we" language to show partnership. Be transparent about timelines and limitations.

### Blog and long-form content

Share real stories and struggles from the journey. Write like sharing with peers, not lecturing. Use first person (I/we) to create connection. Include behind-the-scenes insights.

## Voice references

No specific brands named. The Volkswagen Golf is used as an analogy for the overall tone: practical, does what it's supposed to, no need to be flashy. Implies admiration for brands that are functional, unpretentious, and trusted over aspirational or status-driven ones.

## Voices to avoid

Explicitly not:
- Arrogant or presumptuous ("We're 10 steps ahead of the market...")
- Claiming to be the "all-in-one solution" for everything
- Engineering-led, features-obsessed — the way other headless CMSes sound
- Overly technical or jargon-heavy
- Always running at breakneck pace with tight deadlines
- Talking down to users (pretending to have all the answers)

## Voice in practice — observations from website copy

The homepage copy demonstrates the voice consistently:
- Talks directly to the reader using "you" throughout
- Names the frustration before offering the solution
- Uses conversational asides ("You know the drill.")
- Short declarative sentences alternate with longer empathetic ones
- Features are described by what they *remove* ("No jumping between tools", "No Designer access needed", "No deploy step")
- CTAs are confident and low-pressure ("Just publish")

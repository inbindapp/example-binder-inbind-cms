# ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

> ⚠️ This page was generated from incomplete source material. Review and expand the sections that are missing or thin.

## Primary ICP

A content marketer or content lead at a company whose website is built on Webflow, who is responsible for regularly publishing and updating content — blog posts, case studies, landing pages — and who is blocked or slowed down by the limitations of Webflow's native CMS editor.

> **Note (January 2026):** The team is shifting focus toward content managers at companies directly. See the agency ICP section below for context on why agencies are being de-prioritised.

## Firmographics

- Company size: Small to mid-size (SMBs, startups, scale-ups; also marketing agencies)
- Website platform: Webflow (primary); also Webstudio, Astro, Next.js, Nuxt, or SvelteKit
- Industry: Any, but likely tech, SaaS, agencies, or content-heavy businesses
- Geography: Not specified; English-language product

## Role and demographics

- **Role:** Content Marketer, Content Manager, SEO Manager, Marketing Manager, or agency account manager
- **Technical level:** Non-technical. Does not write code. May not have Webflow Designer access.
- **Team size:** Solo marketer or small content team (1–5 people)

## Day before Inbind

- Opens multiple tabs to manage drafts, check metadata, and track what's live
- Has to ask a developer or designer to publish content or fix a missing SEO field
- Manually copy-pastes SEO titles and descriptions across collection items
- Loses track of which items are missing metadata or aren't ready to publish
- Frustrated that Webflow's editor feels slow for day-to-day content work

## Triggering events

- Publishing a post with an empty meta description and only noticing after
- Waiting on a developer to push something live that was ready days ago
- Scaling to more content volume and hitting Webflow's collection limits
- An agency client breaks something in Webflow Designer while trying to edit a blog post
- Starting a programmatic SEO initiative and having no scalable way to manage it

## Success at 90 days

- Publishing content without opening Webflow Designer or waiting on a developer
- Every live post has complete SEO metadata
- Content health check catches gaps before they go live
- Generated fields handle repetitive metadata composition automatically
- Full visibility into what's ready, what's missing, and what's live

## Common objections

Not fully documented. Likely includes:
- "We already have Webflow, why add another tool?"
- "Is $29/month worth it for just editing?"
- "Will this break our Webflow setup?"

Inbind's responses (inferred from site copy):
- Webflow handles design; Inbind handles content — they complement each other
- Setup takes minutes; existing collections sync automatically; your styles stay intact
- Doesn't replace Webflow Designer, just sits on top of the CMS layer

> ⚠️ Objections should be validated with the team from real sales conversations.

## Bad fit signals

- Primarily a developer or designer who wants to control site structure, not just content
- Using a platform that Inbind doesn't support (e.g., WordPress, Squarespace, Framer)
- Publishing content infrequently (monthly or less) — the workflow gains matter more at volume

## Agency ICP (under review)

**Buyer (Decision-Maker):** Agency founder or lead dev, 30–45 years old. Values time savings and client retention. Budget: $50–200/month per tool.

**User (Daily):** Content specialist or marketer within the agency. Frustrated by Webflow's rigid CMS. Seeks Notion-like editing experience.

| Criterion | ICP Fit | Non-ICP Example |
|---|---|---|
| Team Size | 5–50 | <5 solo freelancers |
| Webflow Usage | 5+ sites/quarter | Occasional builds |
| Client Type | B2B SaaS / tech | E-commerce / retail |
| Pain Level | CMS handoff issues | Design-only focus |
| Budget | $1K+/year on tools | Free-tier only |

**Why agencies are being reconsidered as primary ICP (as of January 2026):**
- Agencies price-compete heavily — adding Inbind's cost into a client deal may not work
- Agencies are often not the end users of the CMS themselves
- Agency engagements are often project-based, not ongoing

Exceptions that still fit: agencies using Whalesync + Airtable combinations, and agencies focused on content creation (not design-only).

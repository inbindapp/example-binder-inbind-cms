# Why Now

## What's changing

AI tools have dramatically accelerated content drafting — but the publishing workflow hasn't kept pace. "AI can draft in seconds, but cleaning up tone, structure, and formatting still eats hours. So you either publish rushed content that feels off-brand, or you slow down and miss windows." The bottleneck has shifted from writing to the gap between a finished draft and a live page. Separately, AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is emerging alongside SEO as a new content discovery channel, raising the stakes for content completeness and structure.

## Why now and not before

Content creation itself was the bottleneck — teams were slow to produce, so publishing friction was just one of many problems. AI has removed the writing bottleneck almost entirely. Now content can be drafted in seconds, which means the next constraint — operations, publishing, quality control — is suddenly the thing holding teams back. The problem existed before; AI made it *the* problem.

## Implications for customers

Content teams are being asked to produce more content, faster, and at higher SEO quality — while still dependent on developer and designer workflows that were designed for a slower publishing cadence. The mismatch between content velocity and publishing friction is growing.

## Our position

The founding team lived this problem directly — Petteri ran a Webflow CMS setup for a real company and built painful workarounds for programmatic SEO before building Inbind. They're not solving a hypothetical. They also built the product on their own stack (now on Webstudio + S3), so they continuously dog-food it.

## Cost of inaction

Chaos → inefficiency → bad marketing → lost opportunity. As content velocity increases, the cost of operational friction compounds: more content in the pipeline means more chances for things to slip through incomplete, inconsistent, or never published.

## The promised land

Content teams focused entirely on strategy and quality — the "What" — while Inbind handles the "How": the operations, publishing, SEO completeness, and eventually the full lifecycle from brief to published piece.
